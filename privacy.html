Privacy policy

Last updated 24 November 2026. This policy covers the Tagger app for Android (package uk.jj.tagger) and this website. Tagger is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom, who can be reached by opening an issue on the project's GitHub page.

The short version

  • Tagger keeps everything on your phone, encrypted. There are no accounts, no servers, no advertising and no analytics.
  • It only goes online for web-request and Wake-on-LAN steps you build yourself, and lists every request.
  • Tag passwords and passphrases are never stored.

What stays on your phone

Tagger keeps your scan history, saved tags, tasks, linked tags, variables, dumps, batch logs, run and check-in logs and your settings in the app's private storage. They are encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a key held in Android's Keystore. History is kept for 30 days unless you choose otherwise. Android's cloud backup and device transfer are switched off for Tagger.

What goes on a tag

Whatever you write to a tag can be read by any phone that taps it, unless you lock it with a passphrase as an encrypted tag. Task tags made by Tagger are signed with a key held on your phone, so Tagger can tell your tags from anyone else's.

Files and backups

Dumps, spreadsheets, QR pictures and backups are only made when you ask, and saved where you choose. Backups are encrypted with a password you set, unless you choose a plain file, which Tagger warns anyone could read.

Keeping and deleting your data

Clear history in the Library or in Settings, and delete saved tags and tasks one by one. Uninstalling Tagger deletes everything it holds.

Your rights

Because the developer holds no data about you, there is nothing to request, correct or erase on a server. Everything is already in your hands.

This website

This site is hosted by GitHub Pages. It uses no cookies and no analytics. The only script on it unlocks the download once the lawful-use box is ticked. GitHub may keep standard web server logs, such as IP addresses, under GitHub's privacy statement.
